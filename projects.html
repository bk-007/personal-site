Selected work · 01—08

Real problems.
Better systems.

Eight examples of how I use leadership, process improvement, quality systems, data, and practical technology to close the gap between intended work and real work.

Generalized by design

The operating problems are real. The confidential details stay confidential.

These are not polished hypotheticals. They are patterns from real work—shared without employer information, product details, or internal manufacturing data.

01People system

Team development · Leadership

Building a capable, accountable quality team.

I built a stronger team culture through clear expectations, regular one-on-ones, meaningful recognition, cross-training, and delegated ownership. The goal was not simply more output. It was better judgment and greater capacity.

  • Stronger ownershipMeaningful responsibility
  • Greater engagementVisible development
  • More capacityCapability beyond one leader

Coaching · Delegation · Performance management · Employee development

02Cross-functional leadership

Mentoring · Applied learning

Seeing potential and giving it somewhere to go.

Some of my most useful leadership work began before anyone formally assigned it. I have helped people outside my reporting line take on real operating problems, test their own ideas, and grow through useful responsibility.

  • Earlier contributionPotential moved into action
  • Practical learningDevelopment through real work
  • Cross-functional trustInfluence without title

Mentoring · Delegation · Applied learning · Influence without authority

03Data system

Manufacturing intelligence

Putting scattered production data in one place.

Production and test data was scattered across files, folders, and disconnected systems. I built an application that linked related work orders, compared runs, calculated key metrics, and generated useful reports.

  • One source of truthLess fragmented file hunting
  • Faster analysisSearch and compare in minutes
  • Better decisionsProcess and test history connected

Python · SQLite · Data pipelines · Desktop UI · Reporting

04Information system

Quality · Traceability

Making quality information easier to use.

I redesigned how quality information moved through the operation—reducing repetitive preparation, linking records, and making the right information accessible to people without requiring them to be data specialists.

  • Less manual workRepetitive preparation automated
  • Stronger traceabilityRecords linked across workflows
  • Wider accessDesigned for nontechnical users

SQL · Power BI · SharePoint · Excel · Process design

05Applied AI

Human-centered technology

Using AI without pretending it is magic.

I use AI to make knowledge easier to access, speed up iteration, and remove low-value work—while keeping human judgment, verification, and accountability where they belong.

  • Reusable knowledgeGuidance at the point of work
  • Faster iterationIdeas move into prototypes
  • Human oversightTools support judgment

Python · Microsoft 365 · Copilot · Prompt systems · Automation

06Capability system

Growth · Standard work

Giving people a real path to grow.

I translated hard-to-see shop knowledge into clearer capability paths, coaching moments, and practical standards so people could understand what growth looked like and build it through real work.

  • Visible growthClear capability progression
  • Distributed knowledgeLess dependence on one expert
  • Greater ownershipPeople learn through doing

Competency design · Coaching · Standard work · Change leadership

07Operating system

Root cause · Manufacturing

Fixing the point where the process actually breaks.

I trace recurring quality problems upstream, connect the right functions, and clarify process ownership so the operation can detect issues earlier and solve causes rather than absorbing symptoms.

  • Earlier detectionProblems found before escape
  • Clearer ownershipProcesses assigned and maintained
  • Cross-functional actionQuality connected to operations

Lean Six Sigma · Root cause analysis · Metrology · Manufacturing

08Rapid problem solving

Prototype · Test · Learn

Turning “someone should fix that” into a real solution.

I turn recurring friction into testable physical and digital prototypes, then shape the answer through frontline feedback and short learning cycles.

  • Shorter feedback loopsPrototype, test, revise
  • Frontline inputUsers shape the solution
  • Lower frictionSmall problems stop becoming permanent

3D printing · CAD collaboration · Scanning · Prototyping

The common thread

Own the gap. Build what closes it.

The answer might be leadership, process, training, technology—or the right combination.
